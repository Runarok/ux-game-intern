### 1. Core Principle: Game Development is Multi-Disciplinary



* Game development = **integration of multiple industries** (art, tech, business, design).

* No single individual can realistically handle full-scale modern game production.

* The “solo developer” idea is possible but **impractical for quality + scale**.

* Success depends on **coordinated teamwork across specialized roles**.



---



### 2. Industry Structure & Team Composition



* Large studios (AAA):



&#x20; * ~25% programmers

&#x20; * ~75% non-technicalsupport disciplines

* Key disciplines include:



&#x20; * Design, Art, Writing

&#x20; * Audio, Music

&#x20; * Programming, Engineering

&#x20; * Playtesting, UXHCI

&#x20; * Marketing, Legal, Finance

&#x20; * Management, HR

* Teams scale based on **game complexity and market scope**



---



### 3. Team Classification



#### 3.1 Non-Technical Teams (Majority)



**Game Designer**



* Defines gameplay, systems, story, progression

* Drives creative direction



**Producer**



* Ensures deadlines, budget, and scope control

* Prevents excessive design changes



**Artists**



* Create assets: characters, environments, animations

* Work through pipeline: concept → modeling → texturing → animation



**Managers**



* Handle planning, resources, coordination

* Connect teams with business, legal, marketing



---



#### 3.2 Technical Teams (Execution Layer)



**Programmers**



* Implement game systems, mechanics, tools

* Work inside game engine



**Level DesignersEditors**



* Build playable environments

* Balance gameplay using testing + UX input



**Audio Engineers**



* Handle music, sound effects, dialogue systems

* Must consider licensingcopyright



**Playtesters**



* Validate usability, bugs, player experience

* Critical for iterative improvement



---



### 4. Reality of Team Scale



* Even “simple” games require **multiple roles + coordination**

* Large titles involve **hundreds of contributors across countries**

* Additional roles may include:



&#x20; * Localization (translators)

&#x20; * Cultural adaptation

* Complexity grows with **realism, simulation, and audience scale**



---



### 5. Inter-Team Communication



#### 5.1 Managers ↔ Teams



* Ensure resources, timelines, quality

* Conduct regular progress tracking meetings



#### 5.2 Artists ↔ Programmers



* Critical dependency

* Assets must meet technical constraints (size, format, performance)

* Poor communication → rework, delays, or asset rejection



#### 5.3 Team ↔ ClientPublisher



* Regular structured meetings

* Communication must be:



&#x20; * Clear

&#x20; * Non-technical when needed

&#x20; * Respectful

* Late design changes from clients should be controlled



---



### 6. Example Insight: Small Game Complexity



Even a simple concept (e.g., object physics simulation) requires:



* Designers → define behavior

* Artists → create models + textures

* Programmers → physics, UI, controls

* Audio → feedback sounds

* Testing → realism + usability

* High realism → increased computation + development time



**Key Insight:**



* “Simple idea” ≠ “Simple execution”



---



### 7. Intra-Team Communication (Within Teams)



#### 7.1 Regular Meetings



* Each member reports:



&#x20; * Work completed

&#x20; * Issues faced

&#x20; * Ideasimprovements

* Followed by discussionquestions



#### 7.2 Work Planning Behavior



* Members define next goals

* Slight overestimation helps avoid idle slowdown

* Transparency prevents bottlenecks



#### 7.3 Communication Rules



* One speaker at a time

* Structured discussion

* Issues raised early



---



### 8. Scope Control & Prioritization



* Most game ideas are **too large for available timeresources**

* Must define:



&#x20; * Core features (must keep)

&#x20; * Secondary features (can be removed)

* Prefer **removing weak features over adding new ones**



---



### 9. Project Management Tools



#### 9.1 Job Board System



* Tasks listed as small cards (name, description, deadline)

* Team members pick tasks independently

* Rules:



&#x20; * Finish before taking new work

&#x20; * Avoid dropping tasks

&#x20; * If dropped → update progress and return task



**Impact:**



* Reduces management overhead

* Encourages ownership and autonomy



---



#### 9.2 Burndown Chart



* Tracks remaining work vs time

* Shows team velocity (speed of progress)

* Effects:



&#x20; * Improves visibility

&#x20; * Boosts motivation

&#x20; * Highlights delays early



---



### 10. Communication System Summary



* Cross-team communication prevents **technical mismatches**

* Internal meetings maintain **alignment and accountability**

* Client communication ensures **business alignment**

* Poor communication = **time loss, rework, budget issues**



---



### 11. Team Dynamics & Mindset



* Teams are intentionally diverse → better creativity

* You will rarely work with like-minded people

* Collaboration quality impacts final product more than individual skill



---



### 12. Career Positioning Insight



* You should identify:



&#x20; * Your preferred role (design, dev, art, etc.)

&#x20; * Your strengths and comfort areas

* Understanding roles helps in:



&#x20; * Better learning direction

&#x20; * Stronger interviews

&#x20; * Clear career path



---



### 13. Key Takeaways (All Things to Remember)



* Game development is inherently collaborative; solo work doesn’t scale

* Majority of work is non-programming (art, design, production, etc.)

* Clear communication between teams is critical to avoid costly mistakes

* Technical constraints must guide creative decisions

* Even small features can require complex coordination

* Regular structured communication keeps teams aligned

* Scope control is essential; cutting features is often necessary

* Task systems (job boards) improve autonomy and efficiency

* Progress tracking (burndown charts) improves morale and accountability

* Diverse teams produce better outcomes than uniform thinking

* Knowing your role in the system is key to long-term growth
