### 1. Core Principle: Software Engineering in Games

* Software engineering = **structured framework for planning, building, and managing software**
* Games are software → require **discipline, planning, coordination**
* Prevents chaos, delays, and misalignment in teams

---

### 2. Project Planning Fundamentals

#### 2.1 Stakeholders

* Anyone affected by the project:

  * Sponsors, users, developers, managers
* Goals are derived from **stakeholder needs**

#### 2.2 Goals (SMART Framework)

* **S** → Specific (clear, non-vague)
* **M** → Measurable (trackable success)
* **A** → Agreed  Achievable
* **R** → Realistic  Relevant
* **T** → Time-bound

**Key Insight:**

* Goals guide direction + boost morale
* Poorly defined goals → confusion and failure

---

### 3. Deliverables

* Concrete outputs tied to goals

#### Must be:

* Specific
* Measurable
* Time-bound
* Assigned to a stakeholder
* Quality-defined

#### Example Structure:

* Task description
* Expected behavioroutput
* Deadline
* Validation method

**Purpose:**

* Break goals into actionable steps
* Provide clarity on workload and expectations

---

### 4. Scheduling

* Defines **when and how tasks are executed**

#### Includes:

* Time estimates
* Resource allocation (people, tools)
* Task priority

#### Constraints:

* Too much time → inefficiency, complacency
* Too little time → stress, poor quality

#### Adjustment Strategy:

* If deadlines fail → **reduce scope**, not quality blindly
* Must communicate changes to stakeholders

---

### 5. Supporting Plans

#### 5.1 Human Resource Plan

* Define roles, responsibilities, hiring needs
* Consider:

  * Skill
  * Availability
  * Team compatibility (temperament matters)

#### 5.2 Communication & Management Plan

* Ensure information flow across teams
* Tools:

  * Meetings
  * Reports
  * Updated schedules

**Insight:**

* Teams forget, misinterpret → communication must be structured

---

### 6. Risk Management (Core Discipline)

#### 6.1 Risk Identification

* Look for:

  * Delays
  * Failures
  * Redundancies
* Must be **specific**, not vague

#### 6.2 Risk Analysis

* Evaluate:

  * Probability (likelihood)
  * Impact (damage)

#### 6.3 Risk Prioritization

* Use **risk matrix**:

  * High probability + high impact → highest priority
  * Low probability + low impact → lowest priority

---

### 7. Risk Handling Strategies

#### 7.1 Mitigation (Prevention)

* Avoid or reduce risk occurrence
* Example: planning, contracts, better hiring

#### 7.2 Monitoring

* Track indicators of risk
* Detect early changes in probability

#### 7.3 Management (Contingency)

* Plan response if risk occurs
* Focus on damage control

---

### 8. Common Software Risks (High Value List)

* Personnel shortages
* Unrealistic timelinesbudgets
* Wrong features or design direction
* Over-polishing (gold plating)
* Constant requirement changes
* External dependency failures
* Performance limitations

**Usage:**

* Use as a checklist to identify risks early

---

### 9. Risk Strategy Insight

* Early identification = more control
* Plans must **adapt continuously**
* Not all risks are preventable → prioritize correctly

---

### 10. Scrum (Agile Framework)

#### 10.1 Core Idea

* Iterative, flexible development
* Focus on **adapting to change**
* Opposite of rigid (waterfall) approach

---

### 11. Scrum Workflow

#### 11.1 Sprints

* Short cycles (2–4 weeks)
* Each sprint delivers usable progress
* After each sprint → evaluate + adjust

#### 11.2 Backlogs

* **Product backlog** → all requirements (priority-based)
* **Sprint backlog** → selected tasks for current sprint

---

### 12. Scrum Meetings

#### 12.1 Sprint Planning

* Define sprint goals and tasks
* Align team capacity with backlog

#### 12.2 Daily Scrum (15 min)

Each member answers:

* What did I do?
* What will I do?
* What issues am I facing?

**Purpose:**

* Synchronization
* Early problem detection

---

#### 12.3 Sprint Review

* Demonstrate completed work
* Gather stakeholder feedback
* Update backlog

---

### 13. Scrum Principles

* Iteration over rigid planning
* Self-organizing teams (no top-down task assignment)
* Continuous adaptation
* Focus on deliverable progress, not documentation
* Consistency in meetings and cycles

---

### 14. Scrum Challenges

* Requires discipline and accountability
* Can feel uncertain due to evolving plans
* Large teams → communication bottlenecks
* Flexibility vs structure must be balanced

---

### 15. Team Dynamics in Software Projects

* Long-term collaboration → friction is natural
* Productivity depends on:

  * Clear goals
  * Good communication
  * Balanced workload
* Emotional stability of team matters as much as skill

---

### 16. Strategic Insight: Planning vs Reality

* Plans will fail or change → expected
* Systems (scrum, risk mgmt) exist to **handle change**, not avoid it
* Adaptability is more valuable than rigid perfection

---

### 17. Key Takeaways (All Things to Remember)

* Software engineering provides structure to complex development
* Goals must follow SMART principles to be effective
* Deliverables convert goals into actionable units
* Scheduling must balance realism and efficiency
* Communication systems prevent confusion and delays
* Risk management = identify, analyze, prioritize, respond
* High-risk areas should be addressed early
* Scrum enables adaptive, iterative development
* Short cycles with constant feedback improve outcomes
* Teams must self-organize and stay accountable
* Consistency in process is critical for predictability
* Flexibility is necessary because change is inevitable
* Team health and coordination directly impact project success
